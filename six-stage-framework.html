Six Stages of
AI Processingfor a CX Pipeline

Modeled on the modern agentic dev stack — spec-driven cycles, enforced TDD, MCP servers for context, auto-issue creation from observability.

A foundation specification for how Customer Experience work gets rebuilt when AI takes over the data-heavy layer. Humans retain the judgment calls; agents do the rest — under a discipline that looks far more like engineering than traditional CX operations.

Premise

AI absorbs the data heavy-lifting, the first drafts, the continuous monitoring, the repeatable cycles. The human keeps three things: choices and trade-offs, meaning-making and framing, and the leadership presence that no system can generate.

The Framework

Six stages, built to compound.

01
Source of truth
versioned · reviewed

Spec-Driven CX Definition

Analog · PRD + acceptance criteria in the repo

Before agents do anything, they share a single formal spec — what good CX looks like in this company, on this journey, for this segment. The spec is the source of truth every downstream automation builds on.

Contents
  • CX Spec — North Star + principles + brand promise + non-negotiables in machine-readable format (YAML/JSON), not PDF.
  • Journey Contracts — defined acceptance criteria per critical journey: what must happen, which metrics count as pass/fail, which touchpoints are mandatory.
  • Persona & Segment Specs — formalised personas with triggers, jobs-to-be-done, behavioural red flags.
  • Versioning — the CX Spec lives in a git-like system. Every change ships as a PR, reviewed by the CX Lead.
Why it matters Without a formal spec, AI agents hallucinate what "good" means and optimise against accidental proxies. The spec is the guardrail for the entire system.
02
Context on demand
not in the prompt

Context MCP Layer

Analog · MCP servers for agents (GitHub, Jira, Sentry)

Agents don't drag context around manually or live inside one giant prompt. They fetch context through MCP servers specialised by CX domain — each with its own scope, access rules, and freshness guarantees.

Core CX MCP Servers
  • voc-mcp — raw feedback: tickets, calls, reviews, surveys, app reviews.
  • journey-mcp — current state of journey maps, touchpoints, funnel metrics.
  • metrics-mcp — NPS/CSAT/CES, correlations, predictive scores.
  • spec-mcp — the current CX Spec, principles, acceptance criteria.
  • stakeholder-mcp — who owns which journey, who approves, who gets informed.
  • experiment-mcp — history of A/B tests, what worked, what didn't, which hypotheses were rejected.
Why it matters Single source of truth, context efficiency, and differentiated access — write to the backlog, yes; modify the CX Spec, no.
03
Signals in
issues out

Continuous Signal Ingestion & Triage

Analog · Crashlytics → auto-created issue

The system pulls signals from everywhere on its own, classifies and prioritises them, and files CX issues in a tracker — by analogy with how Crashlytics auto-creates issues from crashes.

Automated process
  • Multi-source ingestion — agents pull from every channel in near real-time: tickets, calls, reviews, in-app feedback, social, NPS.
  • Classification — journey stage, touchpoint, sentiment, intent, severity, affected segment, root-cause hypothesis.
  • Clustering — not "1000 KYC complaints" but "three clusters: rejection wording, long wait times, document issues."
  • Auto-issue creation — any cluster crossing severity × frequency × business impact becomes a CX Issue with context auto-populated: signal log, affected customers, predicted impact, similar past issues, suggested owner.
  • Deduplication & linking — existing issues get updated, not cloned.
  • SLA & escalation — untouched S1 issues auto-escalate.
Why it matters The CX manager stops spending 60% of their time figuring out what's happening with customers. They arrive to a prioritised backlog with context already attached.
04
Test first
then change

Hypothesis-Driven Investigation

Analog · TDD — test first, then code

Before changing anything in a journey, the agent (or human-in-the-loop) formulates a testable hypothesis and locks in success criteria. Only then does design and implementation begin. Enforced test-first, for CX.

Workflow
  • Hypothesis spec — drafted in the format: "If we change X in journey Y, we expect metric Z to move by ≥N%, measured over period T, in segment S."
  • Success criteria lock-in — pass/fail criteria are frozen before work starts. No post-hoc reinterpretation.
  • Counter-hypothesis check — the agent must generate 2–3 alternative explanations and test them on data. Protection against overfitting to a convenient narrative.
  • Experiment design — sample size, duration, guardrail metrics, stopping rules.
  • Pre-mortem — what could go wrong, and what safeguards prevent it.
  • Human gate — the CX Lead approves the hypothesis and experiment design before execution begins.
Why it matters Eliminates the classic CX bias of "we changed something and NPS went up, probably because of that." Enforced falsifiability.
05
Agents build
humans review

Agentic Execution & Orchestration

Analog · Agents open PRs, run CI, merge

Approved hypotheses are executed by orchestrated agents, each with its own specialisation, passing work down the pipeline. Humans stay in the loop only where judgment actually matters.

Key agents
  • Design Agent — solution variants (copy, UX flow, support script, email sequence, KB article), grounded in the CX Spec and persona context.
  • Content Agent — final artefacts in tone-of-voice, market translations, A/B variants.
  • Rollout Agent — feature flags, segments, staged ramp (10% → 50% → 100%), auto-rollback on guardrail trip.
  • Comms Agent — internal communications, KB updates, internal wiki sync.
  • Training Agent — adaptive micro-learning for frontline when needed, with comprehension checks.
  • Orchestrator — tracks dependencies: no ship until support is trained; no wording change until compliance approves.
Human-in-the-loop checkpoints
  • Approval of design variants — the complex or high-stakes ones, not all.
  • Final sign-off for production rollout.
  • Sign-off on compliance-sensitive changes.
Why it matters Cycle time from "problem detected" to "solution shipped" drops from weeks to days. The CX manager operates as reviewer and orchestrator — never as executor.
06
System learns
every cycle

Continuous Verification & Learning Loop

Analog · Observability + post-mortem + auto-updating runbooks

After rollout, the system doesn't forget. It keeps watching, comparing against the hypothesis, updating its own models, and codifying lessons learned into the spec itself.

What runs continuously
  • Verification agents — monitor success and guardrail metrics in real time. Metric misses trigger auto-rollback or human alert.
  • Hypothesis ledger — every experiment logged: hypothesis, setup, result, what worked, what didn't, confounders. Training data for future agents.
  • Spec auto-update proposals — when an experiment reveals something new about a persona, journey, or principle, the agent drafts a PR to the CX Spec. Human merges.
  • Cross-issue learning — agents spot patterns across issues: "the last three wins came from onboarding flows with simplified wording — suggest a systemic review of all onboarding copy."
  • Executive narrative generation — periodic summaries that are stories, not dashboards. Three changes, impact in revenue and retention, what was learned, what's next.
  • Model retraining — sentiment, classification, and prediction models retrain on validated labels.
Why it matters The system becomes compounding — each cycle faster and smarter than the last. This is what separates "we use AI" from "we built a learning machine."

The full cycle

Six stages, feeding back into the Spec, the Ledger, and the Models — the three things that make the system get better at itself.

The human in the system

Where the Senior CX Manager lives in all of this

Not collector. Not labeller. Not dashboard-builder. Architect of the system that does all three — and chief steward of its integrity.

Owner of the CX Spec
Writes and approves changes to the source of truth. Every PR to the spec requires their merge.
Gate on hypothesis approval
Decides what gets tested — and what doesn't. The judgment call that protects the portfolio from wasted cycles.
Gate on high-stakes rollout
Compliance-sensitive, brand-critical, irreversible changes. Agents don't ship these alone.
Interpreter of systemic patterns
When the agent surfaces five related issues, the human decides whether it's tactics or a signal for a strategic rethink.
Orchestrator of humans
Alignment with product, compliance, risk, and exec team. Politics, trust, and change management — the parts no system handles.
In essence The Senior CX Manager becomes the Tech Lead of the CX system.
  • Writes the spec.
  • Reviews the PRs — hypotheses and experiment results.
  • Handles complex incidents.
  • Develops juniors — agents and team alike.
  • Defends architectural decisions in front of stakeholders.
